Section 01

Program Overview

Bridge condition assessment in the United States operates under two concurrent frameworks — the legacy National Bridge Inspection Standards (NBIS / NBI) and the modernized Streamlined National Bridge Inventory (SNBI) adopted in 2022. Understanding both is critical for interpreting historical data and transitioning to new element-level condition states.

Key Note As of 2022, FHWA officially transitioned to SNBI, replacing the component-level 0–9 ratings with element-level condition states (CS1–CS4). States are at various phases of this transition. All five states analyzed here report under both frameworks for continuity.
NBI Framework

National Bridge Inventory

Established under NBIS (1971), the NBI uses a 0–9 numeric rating system for Deck, Superstructure, Substructure, and Culvert components. A bridge is classified "structurally deficient" if any key element rates ≤4, or "functionally obsolete" if it no longer meets current design standards.

SNBI Framework

Streamlined NBI (2022)

SNBI replaced the "structurally deficient" label with element-level Condition States (CS1 Good → CS4 Severe/Critical). It uses AASHTO CoRe element quantities and calculates a Health Index (HI) per element, enabling more granular asset management and lifecycle cost planning.

Health Index (HI)

Bridge Health Index

HI is a weighted percentage (0–100%) expressing the ratio of current element value to total potential value, summed across all CoRe elements. HI ≥ 80 = Good; 60–79 = Fair; 40–59 = Poor; < 40 = Critical. It directly drives maintenance priority and funding allocation under the Bridge Formula Program.

Section 04

Notable Bridge Failure Cases

The following documented failures illustrate critical inspection shortfalls, the NBI/SNBI ratings at time of failure, root cause classifications, and subsequent rehabilitation or replacement actions taken. These cases directly shaped current inspection standards.

FIU Pedestrian Bridge Collapse — Miami, FL
Florida International University · March 15, 2018 · Sweetwater, Miami-Dade County
FL · Construction Failure

A 950-ton, 174-ft Accelerated Bridge Construction (ABC) pedestrian bridge — using the FIGG Engineers–designed Self-Propelled Modular Transporter (SPMT) method — collapsed onto SW 8th Street (US-41) during post-tensioning operations of the north end diagonal member (Member 11). Six fatalities and eight injuries resulted. An NTSB investigation found diagonal cracking noted in PT node 11 had been communicated to engineers two days prior but deemed non-critical. The failure was attributed to inadequate post-tensioning force, shear failure in the unreinforced PT pocket, and inadequate engineering review during construction.

Fern Hollow Bridge Collapse — Pittsburgh, PA
Forbes Avenue over Fern Hollow Creek · January 28, 2022 · Pittsburgh, PA
PA · Deterioration Collapse

A 447-foot-long deck truss bridge over Fern Hollow Creek collapsed the morning of January 28, 2022, just hours before President Biden visited Pittsburgh to sign the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law. The bridge had been rated Poor (NBI rating 4) for over a decade. The Port Authority bus with passengers was on the bridge at the time; 10 people were injured. The NTSB determined the primary cause was severely deteriorated concrete abutments and end posts, aggravated by inadequate maintenance and deferred repair over multiple inspection cycles.

(Reference Case – Adjacent State) August 1, 2007 · Minneapolis, MN · NTSB AAR-08/03
Reference Case · Gusset Plate Failure

While not in the five study states, this nationally consequential failure directly shaped inspection protocols now applied in all states. Mianus River Bridge Collapse — Greenwich, CT (Reference)
I-95 over Mianus River · June 28, 1983 · Greenwich, CT
Historical Reference

This collapse — caused by a corroded pin-and-hanger assembly hidden inside the bridge structure — was undetectable by standard visual inspection at the time. Three fatalities. The bridge carried 100,000+ vehicles/day on I-95. This case triggered the creation of modern Special Inspection procedures for pin-and-hanger assemblies and was a foundational driver for fracture-critical inspection protocols now active in all five study states.

Legacy & State Impact

NY, PA, and CT immediately launched audits of all pin-and-hanger bridges. FHWA NBIS was revised in 1986 to add Fracture Critical Member inspection categories. New York's Tappan Zee (original) and PA's countless pin-and-hanger deck trusses trace their enhanced inspection programs directly to this event. All five study states maintain active pin-and-hanger registries with 2-year special inspection cycles.

TX Overpass Failures — Hurricane Harvey Scour, 2017
Multiple TxDOT Structures · Gulf Coast Region · August–September 2017

Dozens of smaller county bridges on the Gulf Coast were lost or severely damaged. Total TxDOT bridge repair costs from Harvey exceeded $180 million.

Key Influencing Factors

Traffic Volume (AADT)

High-AADT bridges (>50,000 vpd) require staged construction, lane rental penalties, and often ABC methods to minimize user costs. User cost (delay, VOC) can exceed construction cost on major corridors — a critical driver in NY and CA. FHWA QUEWZ or BenCost tools quantify user impacts.

Bridge Classification & Strategic Importance

NHS bridges, emergency routes, freight corridors (Interstates, US routes), and evacuation routes receive priority funding and accelerated timelines. FL evacuation bridges (I-4, I-75, US-1) have mandated back-up construction timelines for hurricane season preparation.

Environmental Exposure

Marine / coastal exposure (FL, CA coastal, NY harbor) requires stainless steel or epoxy-coated rebar, high-performance concrete with SCMs, and cathodic protection. Freeze-thaw cycles (PA, upstate NY) mandate entrained air concrete and low w/cm ratios. Floodplain regulatory requirements add permitting duration (Corps Section 404, FEMA NFIP).

Material & Labor Market Conditions

Structural steel prices fluctuated ±40% between 2020–2023 due to supply chain disruptions (COVID, tariffs). Concrete and aggregate costs vary regionally. Labor shortages in construction trades are projected to persist through 2027. CA and NY have the highest labor costs nationally; TX has lowest among the five states.

Utility Conflicts & Right-of-Way

Urban bridges (NYC, Houston, Miami, LA, Philadelphia) often have complex utility conflicts — gas mains, water/sewer, electrical conduits, fiber — requiring utility relocation that can add 12–24 months to timelines. R/W acquisition for widened approaches is frequently the critical path item on replacement projects.

Permitting & Regulatory Timeline

Bridges over navigable waters require USCG Section 9 permits (4–12 months). NEPA environmental review (CE, EA, or EIS) ranges from 6 months to 5+ years. Section 106 historic preservation review applies to many PA and NY bridges. Environmental Justice (EJ) analyses under USDOT Order 5610.2c add community engagement timelines.

Section 07

Alternative Route Planning

When a bridge requires extended closure (typically >4 weeks), a formal Detour Route Study must be conducted analyzing alternative routing feasibility, impacts on secondary bridge/road infrastructure, emergency access, freight continuity, and public communication. Below are documented detour frameworks for long-term rehabilitation projects in the five study states.

When Alternative Routes Are Required FHWA Emergency Relief Program (ER) and the Bridge Formula Program both require formal detour route documentation for any bridge closure expected to exceed 48 hours on NHS routes, or 7 days on local routes carrying emergency vehicles. The detour route must be structurally adequate to carry the diverted traffic loads — often triggering load ratings and potential repairs on secondary bridges.
Pennsylvania — Urban Bridge Closures (Pittsburgh / Philadelphia)
Fern Hollow Bridge Replacement · Forbes Ave Corridor · 2022–2023

Forbes Avenue carries ~20,000 vpd through Squirrel Hill and is a primary E–W arterial. The 13-month detour required a comprehensive multi-modal alternative plan.

  1. Primary vehicular detour: Murray Ave → Beacon St → Shady Ave (added ~1.2 mi / 4 min travel time)
  2. Freight restriction: No trucks >2.5 tons on detour surface streets — I-376 designated for commercial vehicles
  3. Port Authority Transit: Route 61 relocated to Penn Ave with temporary stops; added 6-min headway during peak
  4. Pedestrian/bicycle: Temporary multi-use path alongside Fern Hollow Creek authorized through Frick Park
  5. Emergency services: Pittsburgh EMS and Fire pre-positioned resources in Squirrel Hill; added 12-min worst-case EMS response time acknowledged and mitigated
  6. Public communication: City-operated 311 call center; real-time updates on Pittsburgh.gov; VMS signage at 12 locations
  7. Secondary bridge audit: Pittsburgh Public Works load-rated all 6 bridges on detour route; two required temporary weight restrictions
Texas — Rural Highway Bridge Closures (Gulf Coast)
Post-Harvey US-59 Guadalupe River Crossings · 2017–2019

Scour-damaged bridges on US-59 in Victoria County required closures of 6–14 months. Rural Texas context means detours can add 30–60+ miles.

  1. Primary detour: US-77 via Victoria city center (added 28 mi / 35 min) — TxDOT coordinated with Victoria MPO
  2. Emergency access: TxDOT installed temporary Bailey bridge (160-ft modular steel) within 72 hours for emergency vehicle access during initial closure period
  3. Freight continuity: TxDOT issued oversize/overweight permits on US-77 for agricultural and energy sector trucks
  4. USPS / school bus routes: Coordinated with USPS and Victoria ISD for alternative routing — added 45 min to several school routes
  5. Public communication: TxDOT district office media releases; TxDOT DriveTexas website updated in real time; 511 Texas system
  6. Economic impact monitoring: TxDOT and Victoria EDC tracked fuel, freight, and retail impacts during closure; estimated $1.2M/month economic impact on local commerce
New York — Major Bridge Rehabilitation (GWB / Verrazano)
PANYNJ George Washington Bridge Deck Replacement · 2011–2017

The GWB carries 102 million vehicles/year — the world's busiest bridge. Deck replacement required phased work with no full closure, only lane-by-lane staged construction.

  1. No full closure protocol: Lane-by-lane deck panel replacement using prefabricated deck modules — each 8-ft panel replaced in a single overnight window (10 PM–5 AM)
  2. Staged construction: 4 phases over 6 years, maintaining minimum 4 lanes operational at all times on upper level; lower level closed in sectors
  3. Regional detour: NYSDOT / NJDOT coordinated Tappan Zee Bridge and Lincoln/Holland Tunnel as overflow relief — real-time monitoring and traveler info via 511NY and 511NJ
  4. Transit enhancement: NJ Transit added express bus service on GWB Bus Station routes; PATH ridership data monitored for overflow impacts
  5. Freight management: Port Authority partnered with NJTPA on overnight truck diversion to Lincoln Tunnel on major panel nights
  6. Construction scheduling: Work windows avoided Thanksgiving/Christmas peak periods and major stadium events by programmatic scheduling agreement with MTA and local municipalities
Florida — ABC Construction Traffic Management
FDOT I-4 Ultimate Interchange & Multiple Bridge Replacements · 2015–2022

Florida's ABC program allows full bridge replacement over active highways in 48–96 hours via SPMT, requiring intensive short-term traffic management rather than extended detours.

  1. Traffic management plan (TMP): Full interstate closure (full stop or contraflow) for the 48–72-hour SPMT window — coordinated 6 months in advance with local MPO and emergency services
  2. Detour routing: FDOT pre-designates Official Detour Routes with signed alternate paths; parallel SR-408 and SR-528 used for I-4 moves
  3. Real-time ITS: FL511 and FDOT SunGuide TMC managed all DMS, CCTV, and ramp metering to guide traffic during the SPMT window
  4. Emergency services: Pre-positioned FDOT HERO units and FHP troopers at strategic intervals; FDOT coordinated with county EMS for temporary post assignments
  5. Public communication: 30-day advance notice; media partner (TV/radio traffic); highway advisory radio (HAR) at 530 AM within 5-mile radius
  6. Hurricane season coordination: All major moves scheduled Oct–May to avoid hurricane season risk of an incomplete structure being exposed to a storm event
California — Seismic Emergency & Long-Term I-880 / Bay Area Bridges
SF-Oakland Bay Bridge East Span Replacement · 2002–2013 & Seismic Retrofit Program

The Bay Bridge East Span replacement was the longest and most complex of the five-state projects — 10+ years including design, environmental review, political negotiations, and construction. The original eastern span was damaged in the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake.

  1. Parallel structure construction: New eastern span built adjacent to old span while old span remained open — zero closures during construction; final switch-over achieved in a 5-day closure window in Sept 2013
  2. Alternative modes: BART ridership increased by 35–40% during the 5-day closure; AC Transit, San Francisco Ferry expanded service
  3. Freight diversion: Richmond-San Rafael Bridge and Carquinez Bridge designated for commercial freight; Caltrans coordinated with Port of Oakland for advance cargo scheduling
  4. Economic cost of the 2013 closure: Estimated $73 million in user costs and economic impact over the 5-day closure window; Caltrans pre-positioned crews for round-the-clock operations to minimize duration
  5. Seismic retrofit closures (ongoing): Individual bridge column retrofit closures across CA are programmed on 3-year advance planning cycles; each requires 3-month traffic study per Caltrans HDM Chapter 100
  6. Public outreach: Multi-language outreach (English, Spanish, Cantonese, Mandarin, Tagalog) given Bay Area demographics; Caltrans District 4 maintained dedicated project website, Twitter feed, and Bay Bridge Info Line
Framework for Alternative Route Planning — All States A best-practice Alternative Route Planning study includes: (1) Traffic impact analysis — AADT redistribution modeling using TransCAD or VISUM; (2) Structural adequacy review of all detour bridges and pavements; (3) Emergency service response time analysis; (4) Freight & evacuation route continuity; (5) Multi-modal assessment (transit, pedestrian, bicycle); (6) Economic impact estimation (user costs, commerce); (7) Environmental Justice review of impacts on low-income/minority communities; (8) ITS & communication strategy (511, VMS, media, social media). Duration for a full study: 4–8 weeks minimum for simple corridors; 6–18 months for complex metro corridors.

Pennsylvania — Critical Assessment Pennsylvania has the most urgent rehabilitation need of the five states. Over 14% of its 22,762 bridges are rated in Poor condition — the highest percentage nationally among large states. The combination of an aging inventory (median bridge age: 52 years), aggressive freeze-thaw deterioration, and historically underfunded maintenance creates the largest per-bridge backlog. The Fern Hollow Bridge collapse (2022) exemplified the consequence of deferred action. PennDOT has received additional BIF allocations, but the backlog continues to grow faster than available funding at current investment levels.
California — Hidden Risk Factor California ranks 4th on the composite urgency score, but its seismic vulnerability introduces a risk profile that pure HI/condition metrics underrepresent. Pre-1989 bridges not yet seismically retrofitted carry catastrophic failure risk disproportionate to their condition ratings. The CA Seismic Retrofit Program has addressed most NHS bridges, but thousands of local agency bridges remain vulnerable, particularly in high-PGA zones of Southern California and the Bay Area.
Florida — Why the Best Performer? Florida benefits from: (1) Younger average bridge inventory (median age ~32 years), partly due to extensive I-4, I-75, and Turnpike expansion in the 1990s–2000s; (2) Aggressive preventive maintenance program (FDOT's Maintenance Rating Program); (3) No freeze-thaw deterioration; (4) Consistent state funding (~$600M/year for bridge maintenance/replacement). FDOT's pioneer status in ABC and element-level inspection has also enabled cost-effective early interventions.
